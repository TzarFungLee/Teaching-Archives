The Argumentative
Essay ⚖️

Writing to convince — with logic, evidence and structure.

→ arrow keys / swipe to move · P to print

How you are marked

The examiner marks three things — 7 marks each

C

Content

Relevant, well-developed ideas that fully answer the question and engage the reader.

L

Language

Accurate grammar, varied sentences, well-chosen vocabulary — and the right formal register.

O

Organisation

Logical paragraphs, clear development, cohesive ties linking every idea to the next.

Content is what you say. Language is how you say it. Organisation is how it fits together.

Before you write

Pick a clear stance — don't sit on the fence

One-sided essay

You argue for or against, all the way through. Simpler, safer, and perfect when you feel strongly.

Two-sided essay

You examine both sides — but your own position must still be crystal clear by the end.

Why it matters: "Both sides have good points, it depends" with no position of your own reads as having no ideas — and loses marks in Content and Organisation. Choose your side in the introduction and keep it to the end.

The blueprint

Six paragraphs, one job each

Para 1Introduction — hook · background · thesis
Para 2–4Arguments — one idea per paragraph, built with P.E.E.L.
Para 5Counterargument + Rebuttal — the other side, and your answer to it
Para 6Conclusion — restate · summarise · end with power

Learn this order until it is automatic — exam planning time should go on ideas, not structure.

Paragraph 1

Introduction: Hook → Background → Thesis

Did you know the average teenager checks their phone over 100 times a day? ← hook: surprising fact or question

In Hong Kong, schools are now debating whether smartphones belong in the classroom at all. ← background: set up the issue

This essay argues that smartphones should be banned during school hours. ← thesis: your stance in ONE clear sentence

After reading your thesis, the examiner should be able to predict what your body paragraphs will argue.

Paragraphs 2–4

Build every argument with P.E.E.L.

P · Point

Topic sentence: state ONE argument.

E · Evidence

Facts, numbers, examples, expert views.

E · Explain

HOW does the evidence prove your point?

L · Link

Tie it back to your thesis.

Firstly, smartphones destroy concentration. A 2024 study found students take twenty minutes to refocus after a single notification. With lessons only forty minutes long, half of every class can be lost to one buzz. Banning phones therefore directly protects learning time.

The E in PEEL

Support every point with evidence

Four kinds of evidence
  • Statistics — "Over 60% of students admit…"
  • Expert views — "Educational psychologists warn…"
  • Real examples — "When one Sha Tin school trialled a ban…"
  • Logical reasoning — "If X is true, then Y must follow…"
Upgrade your claims

I feel phones are really bad for students.

Research consistently shows phones reduce focus.

Everyone knows social media is terrible.

A 2025 survey of HK teens linked heavy use to poorer sleep.

Paragraph 5

The counterargument — and your reply

1 · State the other side fairly

"Admittedly, some argue that smartphones are useful learning tools, allowing instant research during lessons."

2 · Reply — explain why your side is stronger

"However, this benefit is easily replaced by school tablets with managed access, whereas the distraction phones cause cannot be switched off. The costs clearly outweigh the convenience."

An essay that never mentions the other side looks like it hasn't considered it. Mention it, answer it, move on.

Language bank

Transitions that hold the essay together

Adding arguments

Firstly, · Secondly, · Furthermore, · Moreover, · In addition, · More importantly,

Introducing evidence

Research shows… · According to… · For instance, · In fact, · A recent survey found…

Counterargument

Admittedly, · Some argue that… · Opponents claim… · It could be argued that…

Rebuttal & conclusion

However, · Nevertheless, · Despite this, · The real issue is… · In conclusion, · Therefore,

These cohesive ties are what the Organisation domain rewards.

Register check

Sound like an essay, not a chat

Never

Contractions — don't, can't, it's

Slang & chat — "super bad", "guys", "lol"

"I think… I feel…" everywhere

Because… so… in one sentence (因為…所以 trap!)

Instead

Full forms — do not, cannot, it is

Precise words — "severely harmful"

"It is clear that… Evidence suggests…"

ONE connective: "Because X, Y." or "X, so Y."

Register is marked inside Language — free marks for discipline, no big vocabulary required.

Paragraph 6

The conclusion

In conclusion, the classroom is no place for smartphones. ← restate the thesis in NEW words

They fracture concentration, fuel cyberbullying, and deepen inequality between students. ← summarise your 2–3 arguments in one sweep

If we are serious about education, the phones must stay in the lockers. ← the power ending: short, confident, memorable

Never introduce a new argument in the conclusion — summarise what you have proven and stop.

Before you hand in

Five ways students lose marks

  • No clear stance — the examiner finishes the essay unsure what you believe
  • Opinions without evidence — "I feel" claims with nothing behind them
  • No paragraphing — one solid block with no visible structure
  • Ignoring the other side — no counterargument paragraph at all
  • Casual register — contractions, slang, and the because…so double-marker

Your turn

"Should Hong Kong secondary schools ban smartphones during school hours?"

Clear stance in the introduction · three PEEL paragraphs · counterargument answered
formal register throughout · strong final sentence · about 400 words
