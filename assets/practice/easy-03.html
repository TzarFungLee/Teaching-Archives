Harbour Stream and Hong Kong films

Interview with Leo Wan (local streaming film programmer)

[Adapted interview — Soft chalk RC Practice]

Host: Our guest today is Leo Wan, a film programmer at Harbour Stream, a platform that shows Hong Kong movies online. Leo, why do teenagers still care about Hong Kong cinema? Leo: Because the stories feel close to home. Local films use familiar streets, MTR stations, and school settings. That closeness is not something you always get from foreign blockbusters. Host: How do you choose which films to put online? Leo: We look for clear stories and strong acting. Action films are popular, but we also choose quiet family dramas. Viewers find short local comedies easy to finish after homework. Host: Streaming changed how people watch. What worries you? Leo: Screen time. Many young viewers watch on the bus and skip scenes quickly. Hong Kong cinema needs patience, and short clips alone cannot replace a full story. Host: Do local films have enough money to compete? Leo: Budgets are smaller than Hollywood ones. We share posters online and invite student clubs to watch free first episodes. School support helps more than expensive ads. Host: Will Harbour Stream add a weekly live chat with directors soon? Leo: We hope so. Students enjoy asking questions. However, directors are busy and evening slots are limited. A regular weekly chat is likely only if more schools join as partners.
