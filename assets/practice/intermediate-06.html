Text 2 — Pause, play, and patience

[1] This week ScreenSide sits down with a reader who shapes what teens stream after school. Our guest is Leo Wan, a film programmer at Harbour Stream, a platform that shows Hong Kong movies online. Leo, why do teenagers still care about Hong Kong cinema?

[2] Because the stories feel close to home. Local films use familiar streets, MTR stations, and school settings. That closeness is not something you always get from foreign blockbusters. I grew up on Cantopop soundtracks in old comedies, then drifted into J-pop opening themes on anime nights. I’m a bit of a romantic about Hong Kong screens.

[3] What first hooked you on local film? A muted trailer on the bus, honestly. Volume off, but the camera moves were so polished I was blown away. Later I turned the sound on and the Cantonese dialogue stuck in my mind even when I missed a few slang words.

[4] How do you choose which films to put online? We look for clear stories and strong acting. Action films are popular, but we also choose quiet family dramas. Viewers find short local comedies easy to finish after homework. Hong Kong cinema needs patience, and the best scenes are incredibly catchy in feeling — they stay memorable long after the credits.

[5] Some say streaming only works because of clever marketing. True? Local films can look wholesome: decent people, everyday problems, almost no scandal headlines. I was drawn to that positive, steady mood — that’s the whole point of many Hong Kong stories. Other pop cinema can feel moody or noisy in a different way.

[6] Is it all packaging, then? Uh, hmm. That’s a good question. I suppose… posters help, but they cannot replace a honest script.

[7] Are local actors genuinely talented, or just well sold? Genuinely talented. Hard work shows on camera. Director Pam Yu once told me, “A close-up without truth is empty.” My cousin used to say Hong Kong films were “all made in a factory” for tourists, but she changed her mind after a student screening of a quiet family drama.

[8] Any titles you would recommend this month? Start with Ferry Lights — people call it the king of harbour night stories. Class Dismissed is the hottest school comedy: upbeat and charming. Roof Garden Band is a small rock-band drama with meaningful lyrics about friendship. And Last Train Home talks about loneliness in a way secondary students recognise.

[9] What would you change about streaming if you could? Big platforms control most ad money, so it is hard for independent Hong Kong filmmakers to compete. Some trailers cost a lot of money. Binge-watching can feel addictive and exhausting. Newcomers do not find it easy to enter. Short clips alone cannot replace a full story.

Text 3 — Credits rolled the wrong way

Posted by Leo_Streams22 at 10:05 p.m.

[1] After my ScreenSide chat, Harbour Stream gave me two premiere passes for Last Train Home. I brought my friend Omar, who loves quiet Hong Kong dramas more than any foreign blockbuster. We queued early, shared snacks, and expected a perfect night.

[2] A student short called Promise Stop played first; Omar looked truly moved by the last shot on the MTR platform. Then a director’s cut clip of Roof Garden Band filled the room — funky neon titles, loud cheers. Last Train Home closed the bill. We sat so near the screen that we could see every bead of “rain” on the actors’ faces — wild detail. Their director’s next project will be a chart-topper with critics, I’m sure.

[3] At the meet-and-greet I finally faced the lead actor. My heart pounded. I tried to say the film helped my exam stress, but only a dry “hi” came out. He glanced at his phone, said a flat “Hi,” and told his assistant, “We’re done here.” The assistant gave us a soft, sorry look. Omar shrugged; I felt foolish. Ouch. Never again!
