Light sticks and live stages

Interview with Maya Cheung (idol concert stage show producer)

[Adapted interview — Soft chalk RC Practice]

Host: Today we welcome Maya Cheung, a stage show producer for Hong Kong idol concerts at the Hong Kong Coliseum. Maya, what makes a local idol concert special for teenage fans? Maya: The energy in the room. Fans wave light sticks together, and the idols talk to the crowd in Cantonese. That warmth is not just about loud music. Host: How is a Coliseum show different from filming a music video? Maya: On stage, every second is live. If a dancer slips, you cannot edit it out. We rehearse safety moves for two weeks. The dancers find the timing demanding, but they enjoy the cheers. Host: What is the hardest part of your job? Maya: Budgets. Hong Kong concert budgets are often tighter than big overseas tours. We reuse stage pieces and keep lighting simple but bright. Host: Teens watch concert clips on phones. Does that change your plans? Maya: Yes. Many watch short clips before they buy tickets. So we design a few clear “wow” moments for small screens. Still, a phone cannot replace standing with friends in the hall. Host: Will you produce a full outdoor idol festival in Hong Kong soon? Maya: I would love to, but outdoor stages cost more, and weather is a risk. For now we focus on indoor shows. A large outdoor festival soon is possible only if sponsors help.
