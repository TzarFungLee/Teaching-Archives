Defining Relative Clauses (Essential Info)
The teacher / teaches English / is kind The teacher who teaches English is kind. 🔊
book / table The book __________ (that / which) I borrowed The book that I borrowed is on the table. is on the table. 🔊
boy / bag That is the boy __________ (whose) bag was stolen. That is the boy whose bag was stolen. 🔊
shop / grew up This is the estate __________ (where) I grew up. This is the estate where I grew up. 🔊
drop the pronoun The film (that) we watched was great — can you drop "that"? The film we watched was great. ✓ (the pronoun can vanish when it is the OBJECT) 🔊
Non-Defining Relative Clauses (Extra Info)

📦 Concept Compressed

Defining relative clauses identify which person or thing we are talking about. Non-defining clauses add extra information about someone or something we already know.

Key Differences

Aspect Defining Non-Defining
Commas No Yes ,
Purpose Essential ID Extra Detail
Pronouns Who, Which, That Who, Which (No That)

Choosing the Right Word

Talking about… Use Example
a person who / that the student who won
a thing which / that the bus which goes to Stanley
possession whose the boy whose bag was stolen
a place where the estate where I grew up
a time when the day when we met

Important Rules

  • No commas in defining clauses: The man who lives there...
  • Commas in non-defining clauses: My uncle, who lives there, ...
  • That: Never use that in a non-defining clause.
  • Dropping the pronoun: allowed only in defining clauses when it is the object — "the film (that) we watched" ✓, but never when it is the subject: "the man who lives there" (who must stay).
  • Whose vs who's: whose = possession; who's = who is. Only whose belongs in relative clauses.
