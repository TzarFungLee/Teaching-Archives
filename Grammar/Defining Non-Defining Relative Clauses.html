💡 Key Concepts

  • What are Relative Clauses: Sentences that give extra information about a noun using relative pronouns (who, which, that, where, when, whose)
  • Two Types:
    • Defining (Restrictive): Essential information - tells us which person/thing we're talking about
    • Non-Defining (Non-Restrictive): Extra information - adds details but can be removed without changing the meaning
  • Structure:
    • Defining: Noun + relative pronoun + clause (no commas)
    • Non-Defining: Noun, relative pronoun + clause, (with commas)
  • Key Difference:
    • Defining: NO commas, uses "that" or "who/which"
    • Non-Defining: WITH commas, uses "who/which" (NOT "that")

📋 Relative Pronouns

Relative Pronoun Used For Example
who People (subject) The woman who lives next door is a doctor.
whom People (object - formal) The man whom I met was very kind.
which Things/Animals The book which I bought is excellent.
that People/Things (defining only) The car that I want is expensive.
whose Possession The boy whose bike was stolen is sad.
where Places The park where we met is beautiful.
when Time The day when I graduated was special.

📖 Defining Relative Clauses

Purpose: Essential Information

  • Identifies which specific person or thing we're talking about
  • Cannot be removed without changing the meaning
  • NO commas are used
  • Can use "that" instead of "who/which"

Examples

  • The students who study hard pass the exam. (Which students? The ones who study hard)
  • The book that I'm reading is interesting. (Which book? The one I'm reading)
  • The house which has a red door is mine. (Which house? The one with a red door)
  • The man who called yesterday is my uncle. (Which man? The one who called)

📖 Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Purpose: Extra Information

  • Adds extra details about something already identified
  • Can be removed without changing the main meaning
  • WITH commas (like parentheses)
  • Cannot use "that" - must use "who/which"

Examples

  • My brother, who lives in London, is a teacher. (Extra info about my brother)
  • The Eiffel Tower, which was built in 1889, is in Paris. (Extra info about the tower)
  • Shakespeare, who wrote Hamlet, was English. (Extra info about Shakespeare)
  • My car, which I bought last year, is red. (Extra info about my car)

🔄 Comparing the Two Types

Feature Defining Non-Defining
Purpose Essential - identifies which one Extra - adds bonus information
Commas NO commas WITH commas
Can use "that"? Yes ✓ No ✗
Can be removed? No - meaning changes Yes - meaning stays the same
Example Students who are late must sign in. My students, who are very smart, passed.

🎯 Same Sentence, Different Meanings

Sentence Type Sentence Meaning
Defining My sister who lives in Paris is a lawyer. I have more than one sister. The one in Paris is a lawyer. (The others may not be)
Non-Defining My sister, who lives in Paris, is a lawyer. I have one sister. She lives in Paris and is a lawyer. (Extra information)
Another Example
Defining The students who failed the test must retake it. Only some students failed - those specific students must retake it.
Non-Defining The students, who failed the test, must retake it. All the students failed the test - all must retake it.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

❌ Incorrect ✅ Correct
My brother, that lives in Paris, is a doctor. (can't use "that") My brother, who lives in Paris, is a doctor.
The book, which I'm reading, is good. (not extra info - defining) The book which I'm reading is good.
Students who are late must sign in,. (no comma in defining) Students who are late must sign in.
The man who I saw he was tall. (double subject) The man who I saw was tall.
My sister which lives in London is a teacher. (use "who" for people) My sister who lives in London is a teacher.
Shakespeare who wrote Hamlet was English. (missing commas) Shakespeare, who wrote Hamlet, was English.

📦 Concept Compressed

Defining and non-defining relative clauses (often introduced by relative pronouns like who, which, or that) differ in purpose, punctuation, and pronoun rules. Defining clauses provide essential information to identify the noun, while non-defining ones add extra, non-essential details.

Defining Clauses

These clauses specify or restrict which person or thing is meant, so no commas are used. They often allow that instead of who/which, and the pronoun can be omitted if it's the object.

  • The woman who lives next door is my teacher. (Identifies which woman.)
  • Cells that show abnormality need testing. (Essential detail.)

Non-Defining Clauses

These give additional information and always require commas for separation. Use who for people, which for things (never that), and never omit the pronoun.

  • My teacher, who lives next door, won an award. (Extra info; assumes one teacher.)
  • London, which has great parks, is exciting. (Non-essential.)

Key Differences

Aspect Defining Non-Defining
Commas No Yes
Purpose Essential identification Extra info
Pronouns Who/which/that; omittable as object Who/which only; required
Example Book that I read yesterday (specifies book) This book, which I read yesterday (adds detail)
