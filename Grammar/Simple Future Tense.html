💡 Key Concepts

  • When to use: Future plans, predictions, promises, spontaneous decisions
  • Structure: Subject + will + base verb + (object/complement)
  • Alternative: Subject + be going to + base verb (for planned actions)
  • Examples:
    • I will travel to Japan next year.
    • She is going to study medicine.
    • They will arrive tomorrow.
  • Time markers: tomorrow, next (week/month/year), soon, in the future, later

📦 Concept Compressed

The Simple Future Tense expresses actions or states that will happen in the future. English has two main forms: will and be going to, each with distinct uses.

When to Use

Form Use Example
will Spontaneous decisions, promises, predictions I'll help you. / It will rain tomorrow.
be going to Planned actions, intentions, evidence-based predictions I'm going to study tonight. / Look at those clouds—it's going to rain.

Formation

  • Will: Subject + will + base verb (I will call you.)
  • Be going to: Subject + am/is/are + going to + base verb (She is going to travel.)

Common Time Markers

Tomorrow, next week/month/year, soon, later, in the future, in [future time]

Key Rules

  • Will: Same form for all subjects; contractions: I'll, you'll, he'll, etc.
  • Be going to: Conjugate be according to subject (am/is/are)
  • Negatives: will not (won't) / am/is/are not going to
  • Questions: Will + subject + base verb? / Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + base verb?
