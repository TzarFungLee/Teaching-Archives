Comparative and Superlative Adjectives

💡 Key Concepts

  • Comparative: Compare TWO things (e.g., bigger, more beautiful, better)
  • Superlative: Compare THREE or more things - shows the extreme (e.g., biggest, most beautiful, best)
  • Rules:
    • Short adjectives (1 syllable): add -er / -est
    • Long adjectives (2+ syllables): use more / most
    • Irregular adjectives: good → better → best, bad → worse → worst

📦 Concept Compressed

Comparative adjectives compare two things, while superlative adjectives identify the extreme among three or more. Formation rules depend on syllable count and adjective type.

Formation Rules

Adjective Type Comparative Superlative Example
1 syllable Add -er Add -est tall → taller → tallest
1 syllable ending CVC* Double + -er Double + -est big → bigger → biggest
2 syllables ending -y Change y to -ier Change y to -iest happy → happier → happiest
2+ syllables more + adjective most + adjective beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful
Irregular Unique form Unique form good → better → best
bad → worse → worst

*CVC = Consonant-Vowel-Consonant (e.g., big, hot, sad)

Usage Patterns

Form Pattern Example
Comparative X is [comparative] than Y She is taller than him.
This book is more interesting than that one.
Superlative X is the [superlative] (+ in/of...) He is the tallest student in class.
This is the most interesting book of all.

Common Irregular Adjectives

  • good → better → best
  • bad → worse → worst
  • far → farther/further → farthest/furthest
  • little → less → least
  • much/many → more → most

Key Rules

  • Use "than" with comparatives: bigger than (NOT bigger as)
  • Use "the" with superlatives: the biggest (NOT biggest)
  • Don't mix forms: ❌ more taller, ❌ most biggest → ✓ taller, ✓ biggest
