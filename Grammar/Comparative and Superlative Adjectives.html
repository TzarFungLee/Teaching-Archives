Use them in sentences
tall / class Ka Ming is __________ (tall) than me, but Siu Fung is __________ (tall) student in the class. Ka Ming is taller than me, but Siu Fung is the tallest student in the class. 🔊
busy / MTR The MTR is __________ (busy) at 8 am than at noon. Admiralty is __________ (busy) station of all. The MTR is busier at 8 am than at noon. Admiralty is the busiest station of all. 🔊

📦 Concept Compressed

Comparative adjectives compare two things, while superlative adjectives identify the extreme among three or more.

Formation Rules

Adjective Type Comparative Superlative
1 syllable (tall, cold) Add -er → taller Add -est → tallest
Short vowel + consonant (big, hot, thin) Double it: bigger biggest
2 syllables ending in -y (happy, busy, easy) y → -ier: happier y → -iest: happiest
Longer adjectives (beautiful, expensive) more + adj most + adj
Irregular better / worse / further / less / more best / worst / furthest / least / most

Usage Patterns

  • Comparative: X is taller than Y.
  • Superlative: X is the tallest.

Key Rules

  • Use "than" with comparatives: taller than...
  • Use "the" with superlatives: the tallest...
  • Don't mix forms: ❌ more taller → ✓ taller · ❌ most easiest → ✓ easiest
  • Same level? Use as … as: Kowloon Bay is as busy as Mong Kok. (Negative: not as busy as)
  • Growing change: The days are getting hotter and hotter.
