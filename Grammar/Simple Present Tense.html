What is Simple Present Tense?

✓ Use Simple Present to say:
  • Habits and routines: Things you do regularly ("I drink coffee every morning")
  • General facts: Things that are always true ("The Earth goes around the Sun")
  • Regular actions: Things that happen again and again ("She works on Mondays")
  • Schedules and plans: Timetables and fixed plans ("The bus arrives at 8:00")

💡 Key Concepts

  • When to use: Habits, routines, general truths, permanent situations
  • Structure: Subject + verb (base form / +s for 3rd person singular) + (object/complement)

💡 The Basic Pattern

Subject (WHO) Verb (ACTION) Object (WHAT/WHERE) Time (WHEN)
I, You, We, They study, play, work English, soccer, here every day, always, sometimes
He, She, It studies, plays, works (add -s) English, soccer, here every day, always, sometimes

Examples of Simple Present Tense

✓ Correct Sentences:
  • I study English every day.
  • She works at a hospital on Mondays.
  • The sun rises in the east.
  • They eat breakfast at 7:00.
  • It rains a lot in summer.

⚠️ Important Rule: The -S Ending

❌ REMEMBER: Add -S for he/she/it!
❌ WRONG ✓ CORRECT
She go to school. She goes to school.
He play soccer. He plays soccer.
It rain often. It rains often.

Time Words to Use with Simple Present

These words tell us WHEN something happens:

  • Always / Never: "I always drink water." / "He never eats meat."
  • Usually / Rarely: "She usually arrives early." / "They rarely go out."
  • Often / Sometimes: "It often rains." / "We sometimes cook together."
  • Every + time: "I study every day." / "She works every Monday."
  • On + day: "We have class on Tuesdays."
  • In the morning/afternoon/evening: "I drink coffee in the morning."

📦 Concept Compressed

The Simple Present Tense describes regular actions, habits, general truths, and permanent situations. It uses the base form of the verb, adding -s or -es for third-person singular subjects (he, she, it).

When to Use

  • Habits and routines: I drink coffee every morning.
  • General facts: The Earth orbits the Sun.
  • Permanent situations: She works at a bank.
  • Scheduled events: The train leaves at 9 AM.

Formation

Subject Verb Form Example
I/You/We/They Base form They play tennis.
He/She/It Base + s/es He plays tennis.

Common Time Markers

Always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never, every day/week/year, on Mondays, in the morning

Key Rules

  • Third-person -s: Add -s (works), -es (watches, goes), or -ies (studies) for he/she/it
  • Negatives: Use do not (don't) / does not (doesn't) + base verb
  • Questions: Do/Does + subject + base verb?
