📦 Concept Compressed

Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition. English pronouns change form based on their grammatical function: subject (who does the action), object (who receives it), possessive (ownership), and reflexive (action to oneself).

Pronoun Types

Type Function Examples
Subject Performs the action (who/what does) I, you, he, she, it, we, they
Object Receives the action (who/what receives) me, you, him, her, it, us, them
Possessive Adjective Shows ownership before a noun my, your, his, her, its, our, their
Possessive Pronoun Shows ownership, replaces noun mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs
Reflexive Refers back to the subject myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves

Key Rules

  • Subject pronouns: Come before the verb (She runs. They play.)
  • Object pronouns: Come after the verb or preposition (I see him. Talk to me.)
  • Possessive adjectives: Must be followed by a noun (my book, their house)
  • Possessive pronouns: Stand alone, no noun after (This is mine. The car is theirs.)
  • Reflexive pronouns: When subject and object are the same (I taught myself. She hurt herself.)
