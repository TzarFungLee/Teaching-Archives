One example for each column:
  • They live in Kowloon. (subject — before the verb)
  • Miss Chan helped us. (object — after the verb)
  • The dog wagged its tail. (possessive adjective — needs a noun; no apostrophe!)
  • That seat is theirs. (possessive pronoun — stands alone)
  • We enjoyed ourselves at Ocean Park. (reflexive — subject and object are the same people)

📖 Practice

Choose the pronoun Practice Sentence
Subject or Object?
she / her __________ (she / her) plays badminton, and everyone watches __________ (she / her). She plays badminton, and everyone watches her. 🔊
we / us The coach gave __________ (we / us) a day off, so __________ (we / us) went to the beach. The coach gave us a day off, so we went to the beach. 🔊
Possessive Adjective or Possessive Pronoun?
their / theirs That is __________ (their / theirs) classroom — the one next door is __________ (their / theirs). That is their classroom — the one next door is theirs. 🔊
my / mine This is __________ (my / mine) umbrella. The blue one is __________ (my / mine) too. This is my umbrella. The blue one is mine too. 🔊
Watch out: its vs it's · reflexives
its / it's __________ (Its / It's) raining, so the cat is licking __________ (its / it's) fur indoors. It's raining, so the cat is licking its fur indoors. 🔊
himself My brother taught __________ (?) to play the guitar. My brother taught himself to play the guitar. 🔊

📦 Concept Compressed

Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition. English pronouns change form based on their grammatical function.

Key Rules

  • Subject pronouns: Come before the verb.
  • Object pronouns: Come after the verb or preposition.
  • Possessive adjectives: Must be followed by a noun (my book).
  • Possessive pronouns: Stand alone (This is mine).
  • Reflexive pronouns: Use when subject and object are the same.
