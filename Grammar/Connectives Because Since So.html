She is happy __________ (because) she passed. She is happy because she passed the exam. 🔊 She passed, __________ (so) she is happy. She passed the exam, so she is happy. 🔊
__________ (Since) the typhoon signal was raised, ... Since the typhoon signal was raised, school was cancelled. 🔊 The typhoon signal was raised, __________ (so) ... The typhoon signal was raised, so school was cancelled. 🔊
⚠️ The #1 mistake: never use "because" and "so" together!

In Chinese we say 因為…所以…, but English uses only ONE connective per cause-effect pair:

  • ❌ Because it was raining, so we stayed home.
  • ✓ Because it was raining, we stayed home.
  • ✓ It was raining, so we stayed home.

📦 Concept Compressed

These connectives link cause and effect. Because and since introduce reasons, while so introduces results.

Reason vs. Result

  • Because / Since + Reason, Result
  • Reason, so Result

Key Rules

  • Because/Since: Can start the sentence. If they do, use a comma after the reason.
  • Since vs Because: They mean the same here; since sounds slightly more formal and suits reasons the reader already knows.
  • So: Comes in the middle of two clauses with a comma before it.
  • One connective only: ❌ because … so … — pick one (see warning above).
  • Related lesson: for formal result connectives, see So, Therefore, As a result.
