💡 Key Concepts

  • When to use: Emphasize the action, unknown agent, or formal writing
  • Structure: Subject + 'be' verb + past participle + (by + agent)
    • From Active voice: Subject performs the action (The teacher explains the rule).
    • To Passive voice: Subject receives the action (The rule is explained by the teacher).
  • Reminders: Remember to use the correct form of pronouns!

📦 Concept Compressed

The Passive Voice shifts focus from who performs an action (the agent) to what receives it (the object). It uses a form of be + past participle, with the agent optionally mentioned using by.

Formation

Voice Structure Example
Active Subject + Verb + Object The chef cooks the meal.
Passive Object + be + past participle (+ by agent) The meal is cooked (by the chef).

When to Use Passive Voice

  • Unknown agent: My car was stolen. (We don't know who stole it)
  • Unimportant agent: English is spoken in many countries. (Focus on English, not speakers)
  • Obvious agent: The suspect was arrested. (Obviously by police)
  • Formal/scientific writing: The experiment was conducted in 2020.
  • Emphasis on action/receiver: The Mona Lisa was painted by Leonardo da Vinci.

Passive Forms Across Tenses

Tense Active Passive
Simple Present They clean the room. The room is cleaned.
Simple Past They cleaned the room. The room was cleaned.
Simple Future They will clean the room. The room will be cleaned.
Present Continuous They are cleaning the room. The room is being cleaned.
Present Perfect They have cleaned the room. The room has been cleaned.

Key Rules

  • Use correct form of "be": Match tense and subject (is/are/was/were/will be, etc.)
  • Past participle: Regular verbs add -ed; irregular verbs vary (write→written, make→made)
  • Agent "by + noun": Optional; include only if relevant
  • Object pronouns: When converting pronouns: I→me, he→him, she→her, they→them
