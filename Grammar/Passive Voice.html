📦 Concept Compressed

The Passive Voice shifts focus from who performs an action (the agent) to what receives it (the object). It uses a form of be + past participle, with the agent optionally mentioned using by.

Formation

  • Structure: Subject + be + past participle + (by + agent)

When converting from Active to Passive voice, the Subject and Object swap positions:

  • Active voice: Subject performs the action (The chef cooks the meal).
  • Passive voice: Subject receives the action (The meal is cooked by the chef).

When to Use Passive Voice

  • Unknown agent: My car was stolen. (We don't know who stole it)
  • Unimportant agent: English is spoken in many countries. (Focus on English, not speakers)
  • Obvious agent: The suspect was arrested. (Obviously by police)
  • Formal/scientific writing: The experiment was conducted in 2020.
  • Emphasis on action/receiver: The Mona Lisa was painted by Leonardo da Vinci.
