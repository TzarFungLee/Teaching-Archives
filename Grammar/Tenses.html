💡 Simple Present Tense

  • When to use: Habits, routines, general truths, permanent situations, scheduled events
  • Structure: Subject + verb (base form / +s for 3rd person singular) + (object/complement)
  • Examples:
    • I study English every day.
    • She works at a hospital.
    • The sun rises in the east.
  • Time markers: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day/week/month, on Mondays

💡 Simple Past Tense

  • When to use: Completed actions in the past, past habits, historical events
  • Structure: Subject + verb (past form / -ed or irregular) + (object/complement)
  • Examples:
    • I visited London last year.
    • She wrote a letter yesterday.
    • They lived in Japan in 2010.
  • Time markers: yesterday, last (week/month/year), ago, in (year), when I was young

💡 Simple Future Tense

  • When to use: Future plans, predictions, promises, spontaneous decisions
  • Structure: Subject + will + base verb + (object/complement)
  • Alternative: Subject + be going to + base verb (for planned actions)
  • Examples:
    • I will travel to Japan next year.
    • She is going to study medicine.
    • They will arrive tomorrow.
  • Time markers: tomorrow, next (week/month/year), soon, in the future, later

💡 Present Continuous Tense

  • When to use: Actions happening now, temporary situations, future arrangements
  • Structure: Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing + (object/complement)
  • Examples:
    • I am studying English right now.
    • She is working on a project.
    • They are playing soccer at the moment.
  • Spelling rules: drop final 'e' (make→making), double consonant (run→running), change 'ie' to 'y' (lie→lying)
  • Time markers: now, right now, at the moment, currently, today, this week

💡 Past Continuous Tense

  • When to use: Actions in progress at a specific time in the past, interrupted actions, parallel actions
  • Structure: Subject + was/were + verb-ing + (object/complement)
  • Examples:
    • I was studying at 8pm last night.
    • She was reading a book when I called.
    • They were playing soccer while we were watching.
  • Common with: when (for interruption) and while (for parallel actions)
  • Time markers: at (time) yesterday, while, when, as, all day/night/morning
