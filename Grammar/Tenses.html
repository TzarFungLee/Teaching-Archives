💡 Simple Present Tense

  • When to use: Habits, routines, general truths, permanent situations, scheduled events
  • Structure: Subject + verb (base form / +s for 3rd person singular) + (object/complement)
  • Examples:
    • I study English every day.
    • She works at a hospital.
    • The sun rises in the east.
  • Time markers: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day/week/month, on Mondays

💡 Simple Past Tense

  • When to use: Completed actions in the past, past habits, historical events
  • Structure: Subject + verb (past form / -ed or irregular) + (object/complement)
  • Examples:
    • I visited London last year.
    • She wrote a letter yesterday.
    • They lived in Japan in 2010.
  • Time markers: yesterday, last (week/month/year), ago, in (year), when I was young

💡 Simple Future Tense

  • When to use: Future plans, predictions, promises, spontaneous decisions
  • Structure: Subject + will + base verb + (object/complement)
  • Alternative: Subject + be going to + base verb (for planned actions)
  • Examples:
    • I will travel to Japan next year.
    • She is going to study medicine.
    • They will arrive tomorrow.
  • Time markers: tomorrow, next (week/month/year), soon, in the future, later

💡 Present Continuous Tense

  • When to use: Actions happening now, temporary situations, future arrangements
  • Structure: Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing + (object/complement)
  • Examples:
    • I am studying English right now.
    • She is working on a project.
    • They are playing soccer at the moment.
  • Spelling rules: drop final 'e' (make→making), double consonant (run→running), change 'ie' to 'y' (lie→lying)
  • Time markers: now, right now, at the moment, currently, today, this week

💡 Past Continuous Tense

  • When to use: Actions in progress at a specific time in the past, interrupted actions, parallel actions
  • Structure: Subject + was/were + verb-ing + (object/complement)
  • Examples:
    • I was studying at 8pm last night.
    • She was reading a book when I called.
    • They were playing soccer while we were watching.
  • Common with: when (for interruption) and while (for parallel actions)
  • Time markers: at (time) yesterday, while, when, as, all day/night/morning

📦 Concept Compressed

English tenses express when an action happens (time) and its aspect (completion, duration, or relation to other events). The main time frames are past, present, and future, each with simple, continuous (progressive), perfect, and perfect continuous forms.

The 12 Main Tenses

Tense Structure Use Example
PRESENT
Simple Present base verb (+s for he/she/it) Habits, facts, routines She works every day.
Present Continuous am/is/are + verb-ing Actions happening now, temporary She is working right now.
Present Perfect have/has + past participle Past actions with present relevance She has worked here for 5 years.
Present Perfect Continuous have/has been + verb-ing Actions started in past, continuing now She has been working since 9 AM.
PAST
Simple Past verb-ed / irregular past Completed past actions She worked yesterday.
Past Continuous was/were + verb-ing Actions in progress in the past She was working at 5 PM.
Past Perfect had + past participle Actions completed before another past action She had worked there before moving.
Past Perfect Continuous had been + verb-ing Duration before another past action She had been working for 2 hours when I called.
FUTURE
Simple Future will + base verb Future predictions, decisions She will work tomorrow.
Future Continuous will be + verb-ing Actions in progress at a future time She will be working at 5 PM tomorrow.
Future Perfect will have + past participle Actions completed before a future time She will have worked 10 years by 2025.
Future Perfect Continuous will have been + verb-ing Duration up to a future point She will have been working for 5 hours by then.

Choosing the Right Tense

  • Simple: General facts, complete actions (I eat breakfast every day. I ate breakfast yesterday.)
  • Continuous/Progressive: Actions in progress, temporary situations (I am eating breakfast now.)
  • Perfect: Completed actions with a connection to another time (I have eaten breakfast already.)
  • Perfect Continuous: Duration and continuity emphasized (I have been eating for 20 minutes.)

Key Time Markers

  • Simple Present: always, usually, often, every day, on Mondays
  • Simple Past: yesterday, last week, ago, in 2010
  • Simple Future: tomorrow, next week, soon, will
  • Present Perfect: already, yet, just, ever, never, for, since
  • Continuous: now, right now, at the moment, currently
